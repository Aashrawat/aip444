It says AI systems can be manipulated by attacks such as evasion and poisoning, that NIST’s new guidance categorizes these attacks and mitigation strategies, and that there is no foolproof defense.

## Claim verification
### Main claims in the article
1. AI systems can malfunction when exposed to untrustworthy data and attackers can exploit this.
2. NIST published guidance that identifies types of adversarial machine learning attacks and mitigation approaches.
3. There is no foolproof defense against these attacks.

### What I found
- The linked NIST CSRC publication, **Adversarial Machine Learning: A Taxonomy and Terminology of Attacks and Mitigations**, directly supports the article’s summary.
- The CSRC page lists the authors and abstract and states the report surveys the adversarial machine learning literature and defines attack/mitigation terminology.
- Independent reporting also summarized the same NIST findings, including the claim that attacks such as evasion and poisoning are real concerns and that robust defenses are still limited.
